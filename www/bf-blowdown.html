Tapping through the blowdown

  • Reduced blast means a low melting rate, so gaps are long — tap when liquid has collected, not on the normal clock.
  • T0 − 12 h to T0: cast on the normal schedule; the hearth must go into the blowdown dry.
  • T0 − 4 h: stop injection and oxygen; take a full cast to dry the hearth.
  • During the blowdown: a cast may run dry early when little has collected — fine; close and wait for the next gap.
  • Final cast at target stockline: drill and HOLD the taphole open until it blows gas — do not plug. Stop spray, then blast, then purge.
  • Deep (reline): after blast off, tap the salamander through a hole 0.3–0.6 m below the normal taphole.

      Why the key settings are what they are

      Blast while the stockline is in shaft / belly / bosh / below bosh

      The blowdown blast, as a fraction of normal, is stepped down as the stockline falls. Blast stays ON so the coke keeps burning and the burden keeps descending, but full blast in a shortening column causes three problems that get worse the lower the stockline goes:

      1. Top gas temperature. Less burden above the tuyeres absorbs less heat, so the top runs hotter. Less blast = less gas = less heat carried up, so the top is easier to hold with nitrogen and spray. This is the main reason.

      2. Channelling and slips. A short, uneven column is unstable; high blast pushes gas up preferred paths and the burden can hang then slip.

      3. Blowing material out of the top. High velocity in a near-empty furnace throws coke and dust into the gas system — just when water is going into the gas.

      Trade-off: lower values = slower, cooler, less spray water; higher = faster but hotter and more water-hungry.

      Coke-up of the blowdown burden (+15% deep / +10% partial)

      Extra coke in the burden before charging stops so the furnace empties with a coke-rich column. Extra coke carries heat to the hearth and keeps the shortening column open so gas escapes and liquids drain. The worst outcome is a cold, frozen hearth at the final cast; the coke-up is the main defence.

      +15% roughly replaces the injection stopped at T0 − 4 h. Higher is NOT safer — more coke to burn away means a longer blowdown, more hours at the top limit and more spray water. "+25% to be safe" is a classic mistake. Partial needs less (+10%) because the bosh and hearth stay full of hot burden.

      The coke rate per hour does not change: the coke-up is made by taking ore out of each charge. Start about 12 h before T0 so the coked-up material fills the furnace by the time charging stops.

      Spray water & nitrogen priority

      Nitrogen first, spray second. N₂ cools the gas by dilution without adding H₂. Spray water partly reaches the hot burden and makes H₂ (C + H₂O → CO + H₂). Hold the top with N₂ as long as you can, then spray in small steps.

      Stop rule: H₂ above 12% → stop spray, gas to bleeder. If spray is at capacity and the top is still above limit, cut blast — do not chase water.

      Gas route and the dry GCP bag limit

      Bag cloth burns above its rated temperature. With a dry GCP the gas leaves for the bleeder when the stockline reaches the belly top; with a wet GCP it stays until the bosh top and is held to the top equipment limit.

      Final cast & salamander

      At the target stockline drill a full-depth cast and hold the taphole open — when it blows gas, the hearth is dry. Do not plug it. Stop spray, then blast, then back-draught and purge. For a reline, tap the salamander through a hole 0.3–0.6 m below the normal taphole after blast off. Keep the salamander area bone-dry — water under molten iron explodes.
